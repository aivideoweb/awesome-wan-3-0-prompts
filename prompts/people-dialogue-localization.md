# People, dialogue, and localization — 10 Wan 3.0 prompts

Original prompts for believable speaking turns, multilingual delivery, accessible communication, documentaries, oral history, podcasts, and localized edits. Keep dialogue short enough for natural pauses.

## 01｜Bilingual museum welcome with clean speaker turns

**Mode:** T2V · **Suggested:** 15 seconds, 16:9, dialogue

```text
Inside a contemporary local-history gallery, a guide welcomes two visitors while standing beside a tactile map. 0–5s: medium shot; the guide looks at the visitors and says in natural English, “Welcome. You can begin with the map beside me.” 5–9s: the guide gestures once toward the map; both visitors stay silent and follow the gesture with their eyes. 9–15s: cut to a closer angle without crossing the eyeline; the guide repeats in Mandarin Chinese, “欢迎，请先从我身边的触摸地图开始。” One visitor nods only after the sentence ends. Quiet gallery ambience, soft footsteps, no music under speech. Preserve faces, clothing, positions, map geometry, left-right orientation, and voice identity across the cut. No subtitles, overlapping speech, mouth movement from listeners, invented exhibits, logos, text artifacts, exaggerated gestures, or camera crossing the 180-degree line.
```

## 03｜Sign-language evacuation briefing

**Mode:** I2V · **Suggested:** 16 seconds, 16:9

```text

The presenter stands on the left and speaks two short sentences at a measured pace; the sign-language interpreter stands on the right with both hands fully visible from waist to head. 0–7s: presenter says, “When the alarm sounds, leave by the nearest marked exit.” 7–9s: both pause while the camera gently pushes toward the map. 9–16s: presenter says, “Meet at the tree across the courtyard.” The interpreter signs continuously with natural facial grammar, beginning just after each spoken phrase and finishing before the next. Staff in the foreground watch silently. Preserve hand anatomy, identity, clothing, map, exit arrows, framing, and speaker positions. Clear speech, no music. No cropped hands, invented or gibberish signs, overlapping dialogue, subtitles covering hands, flashing lights, panic, running, text changes, or role swapping.

## 04｜Documentary voiceover with a silent subject

**Mode:** T2V · **Suggested:** 18 seconds, 2.39:1

```text
Create a quiet observational documentary portrait of an elderly bicycle repairer opening a small workshop at dawn. Shot 1: hands unlock a corrugated shutter and lift it; the repairer does not speak. Shot 2: they wipe yesterday’s dust from a wooden bench, place three tools in order, and pump one tire. Shot 3: a shaft of sunlight reaches the spinning wheel as it slows. A separate adult narrator speaks off camera in restrained Japanese: “この店では、急ぐことより、長く使うことを大切にしている。” The subject’s mouth remains closed throughout and their natural breathing is visible only in the shoulders. Camera uses patient locked frames and one slow lateral slide. Sound: shutter, cloth, tools, pump, morning birds beneath the narration. Preserve workshop layout, tool order, clothing, hands, bicycle, and dawn light direction. No lip-sync on subject, sentimental music, subtitles, brand marks, staged eye contact, tool teleportation, or younger-face transformation.
```

## 07｜Transparent newsroom correction segment

**Mode:** T2V · **Suggested:** 14 seconds, 16:9, dialogue

```text
Create a fictional local-news correction delivered calmly by one presenter at a clean studio desk. 0–3s: wide shot establishes the presenter and a blank blue side panel with no readable text. 3–11s: medium shot; the presenter looks into lens and says in natural English, “A figure in our earlier report was incorrect. The meeting begins at six thirty, not six.” They pause briefly after “incorrect” and emphasize only the two times. 11–14s: the presenter lowers their notes, gives one neutral nod, and the camera holds. Voice is direct and unhurried; room is silent except for subtle studio ambience. Preserve face, hairstyle, jacket, papers, desk geometry, gaze, and lighting. No dramatic alert music, fake station logo, ticker, subtitles, changing numbers on screen, smile during the correction, repeated sentence, extra presenter, jump cut, or political branding.

## 08｜Pronunciation lesson with visible minimal pairs

**Mode:** I2V · **Suggested:** 16 seconds, 9:16

```text
Animate the supplied vertical classroom frame. The language teacher demonstrates the English minimal pair “ship” and “sheep” for adult learners. 0–5s: teacher points to the left blank card, says “ship” once at natural speed, then repeats slowly while the camera stays on the full face. 5–10s: teacher points to the right blank card, says “sheep” once and repeats slowly, holding the longer vowel without exaggerating jaw motion. 10–16s: teacher alternates “ship, sheep” once, then stays silent while two students repeat off camera in order. Keep lips fully visible, hands below the chin, and audio clean. Preserve teacher identity, wardrobe, card positions, classroom, and camera. The cards remain blank to avoid text-generation artifacts. No subtitles, phonetic symbols, distorted mouth, tongue close-up, learner faces speaking out of sync, background music, accent mockery, extra repetitions, or camera cuts during a word.
```

## 10｜Laundromat apology micro-drama

**Mode:** T2V · **Suggested:** 20 seconds, 16:9

```text
Set a restrained two-person micro-drama in a quiet laundromat at night. A student in a navy raincoat has accidentally moved a baker’s folded apron from a dryer. 0–5s: the student notices the baker approaching, holds the apron carefully with both hands, and says in Korean, “죄송해요. 제 빨래인 줄 알았어요.” 5–10s: the baker glances from apron to student, keeps their mouth closed, and exhales; dryer drums turn behind them. 10–15s: the baker replies, “괜찮아요. 깨끗한 의자에 놓아 주세요.” 15–20s: the student places the apron on the indicated chair, smooths it once, and both give a small nod. Camera: static wide, slow push to medium during the pause, no reverse angle. Sound: dryer hum, rain on glass, two clear voices. Preserve faces, clothing, apron, chair, spatial positions, eyelines, and dialogue order. No subtitles, crosstalk, melodramatic crying, aggressive gesture, floating laundry, machine-door changes, logo, or mouth movement from the listener.
```
