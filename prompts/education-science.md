## 01｜How street trees cool a summer block

**Mode**: T2V / Edit　**Suggested**: 15 seconds, 16:9

[Before copying: adapt these target settings](../guides/videoweb-workflow.md#adapt-settings)

```text
15 seconds, 16:9, accessible science explainer comparing two adjacent fictional city blocks at noon: one with mature street trees, one without. Preserve the same sun position, road width, building materials, traffic level, and camera height.

00:00–00:05: Split-screen aerial descends evenly across both blocks. On the treeless side, hard sunlight reaches pavement; on the shaded side, leaf shadows move naturally. Use a simple warm-to-cool color overlay as explanatory graphics, not fake thermal-camera footage.
00:05–00:10: Street-level match cut. Show water leaving leaf pores as subtle translucent particles and rising air as slow arrows; keep the real trees visible beneath the overlay. A pedestrian waits in each block for scale.
00:10–00:15: Return to the paired wide view as a breeze moves through both streets. End on the practical contrast: shade covers the sidewalk and a bench, while building geometry remains identical.

Audio: cicadas, light traffic, leaves, calm narrator: “Shade reduces direct solar heating; evaporation moves additional heat into the air.” No exact temperature claim, magic cooling, instant tree growth, misleading weather change, logo, or watermark.
```
